# Reading copies and source authority

Use quality-checked Markdown for searching, close reading, paraphrasing, and
ordinary claim checks. Cite the original publication, not its conversion.
The original PDF remains the canonical local source for identity and extraction
discrepancies. This determines authority, not which file must be opened first.

Read configured locations from the project's `THESIS.md` or existing project
instructions. A literature corpus, bibliography, writing guide, example thesis,
supervisor comments, and manuscript may live in different directories or repos.
Resolve relative paths from the project brief. Do not search a former author's
home directory or assume private guidance is bundled with this skill.

## When to open the PDF

Check the relevant passage or page when relying on:

- Exact quotations, numerical results, equations, tables, or figures.
- Footnotes, annotations, page-specific information, or layout lost in extraction.
- Incomplete, malformed, conflicting, or contextually ambiguous text.
- Uncertain bibliographic details or reference mappings.

Inspect the rendered page when OCR or the embedded text layer is doubtful;
comparing two extractions of faulty text is not independent verification.
Use publisher metadata when the PDF cannot resolve identity. Record unresolved
uncertainty rather than guessing. OCR in a converter does not establish how the
original PDF was produced.

Conversion alone does not prove QA passed. Representative checks support normal
use of the reading copy; they do not establish a measured accuracy rate. Record
known extraction defects and check affected passages locally. Do not reconvert
an entire collection for a small writing task.

Reuse checks while the claim scope, source version, and relevant extraction
remain unchanged. A wording edit does not invalidate a check. A changed value,
population, interpretation, or damaged passage can.

## Writing feedback versus research evidence

Read actual feedback when available. A supervisor's suggested claim, numerical
threshold, or novelty statement is a lead to verify, not supporting evidence.
Example theses illustrate techniques; their chapter order, pronouns, or density
are not mandatory. Follow current project requirements and the author's voice.

The reusable writing guidance was adapted from the maintainer's thesis workflow,
informed by Jan van Gemert's research/writing/presentation guidance and Simon
Peyton Jones's *How to write a great research paper*. Original guides and private
annotated manuscripts are not included. Users supply their own project sources;
this skill does not claim access to them.
