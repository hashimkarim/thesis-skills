# Thesis research and evidence

Read applicable project instructions and `THESIS.md`, when present, resolving
its paths from that file. Establish the discipline and actual evidence available.
Do not impose an experimental engineering template on archival, theoretical,
qualitative, or mixed-method research.

## Question to evidence

Make the central research question explicit and connect subquestions to it.
For each claim, identify the observation or argument that could support it and
what would count against it. Challenge high-risk assumptions early with the
smallest useful study or proof of concept. Published findings do not automatically
generalize to the thesis setting.

For empirical work, specify the question, unit of analysis, data or participants,
procedure, baseline, measures, expected evidence, and limitations before treating
an outcome as a result. Distinguish exploratory from confirmatory analysis.
Follow applicable institutional requirements for participant work and data use;
do not fabricate approval or imply a writing plan authorizes recruitment.

## Fair comparisons

- Explain the comparison's purpose before its configuration.
- Isolate variables when estimating a causal effect. Use an appropriate
  factorial or other design when interactions or confounders matter; changing
  one variable is not a universal design rule.
- Keep evaluation conditions comparable. Identify data leakage, test-set tuning,
  unequal budgets, and fallback behavior that changes the actual treatment.
- In predictive studies, separate fitting, selecting settings, and final testing.
  Multiple fold-specific choices are not inherently invalid; inspect independence
  and the separate decision needed for deployment.
- Explain metrics through the errors or phenomena they measure, including units,
  uncertainty, aggregation, sample size, and missing data where relevant.

## Interpret what was observed

Keep question, setup, result, and interpretation close together. State the main
pattern and its consequence rather than narrating every table entry. Distinguish
statistical evidence from practical importance and association from causation.

Separate boundaries in a measurement chain: a model output, system action,
physical effect, and participant response require different evidence. Better
model performance alone does not establish better user outcomes. Label proposed
work, implemented features, simulations, device tests, and completed studies
according to the artifacts actually inspected. A code fix is not a rerun.

If a flawed comparison is found, mark affected conclusions and the correction
needed. Preserve raw evidence. Report limitations beside the claims they limit,
including plausible failure conditions and external validity. Return a concrete
study plan or evidence review within the requested scope.
