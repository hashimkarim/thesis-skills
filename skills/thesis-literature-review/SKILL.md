# Thesis literature review

Read the project's `THESIS.md` and applicable agent instructions if available.
Resolve paths relative to the brief. Establish the research question, discipline,
scope, and requested deliverable before searching. Use existing collections and
bibliographies first; do not assume a citation manager, subscription, or API key.

## Search and select

- Derive search terms from the question, competing terminology, methods, and
  relevant populations or settings. Record queries, databases, dates, and
  selection criteria when reproducibility is part of the task.
- Distinguish a scoped narrative review from a systematic review. Do not claim
  exhaustive coverage without a documented search and screening procedure.
- Inspect primary sources. A search snippet, abstract, or another paper's
  citation can identify a candidate but may not establish its detailed findings.
- Identify duplicate versions and use stable DOI/publisher identifiers where
  verified. Preserve existing citation keys. Record inaccessible sources and
  exclusion reasons instead of inventing their contents.

## Read for the thesis question

Use clear, quality-checked Markdown passages for normal reading. Verify exact
quotations, numbers, equations, unclear mappings, and extraction defects against
the source PDF or publisher. Preserve a mapping between reading copy and source.

For each relevant work record only useful fields: question, setting, method,
comparison, result, limitations, evidence location, and relevance to the thesis.
The [reading-note template](assets/reading-note.md) is optional. Distinguish the
authors' claims from your inference. A blank or unknown table cell is preferable
to an unsupported tick.

## Synthesize

Organize around ideas, disputes, and comparison criteria rather than one
paragraph per paper. Explain the criteria before a comparison table. Compare
like with like and make differences in data, assumptions, protocols, and metrics
visible. Give prior work fair credit, including strengths that weaken the
proposed novelty claim.

Ground the gap in what the reviewed evidence does and does not establish.
Avoid unsupported claims that nobody has studied something. Connect the gap to
a research question the thesis can actually address. Return the requested
synthesis, supporting source locations, and unresolved coverage or access gaps.
Do not download paywalled material through unauthorized routes or redistribute
the user's PDF corpus when sharing the skill.
